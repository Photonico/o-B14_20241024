# Thesis figures

Open `figures_for_thesis.ipynb` to adjust the figures. Run it from top to bottom using Python with NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib, h5py, PyYAML, PyMuPDF, Pillow and IPython. Its first cell controls shared typography and colours; each figure group exposes its actual plotting code, canvas size, subplot spacing and legends. Figures display before being saved. The notebook works from either this directory or the repository root and writes PDFs here before copying them to the sibling `PhD_thesis_20251216/figures_proj2` directory. Saved image outputs are cleared to keep the notebook small. No first-principles calculation is rerun; no backup or duplicate raw archive is created.

The `.py` files retain the original batch baseline: `python figures_for_thesis/make_figures.py` regenerates the numerical figures, while `python figures_for_thesis/structures.py` regenerates the structural artwork. Running these scripts will overwrite PDF changes made through the notebook; use the notebook as the editing entry point and only run the batch baseline when that reset is intended. The batch scripts do not read later notebook edits.

The notebook's first cell and the baseline `style.py` start with the same typography: 13 pt axis labels, 11 pt ticks, legends and rounded panel tabs, 1.5 pt lines, serif text and Computer Modern mathematics. Canvas widths follow the thesis insertion widths: 6 in at 0.6 text width, 7 in at 0.7 text width, 8 in at 0.8 text width, and 10 in at full text width. This restores the original distinction between small individual plots and larger figure groups while keeping the printed font size consistent. Heights and margins are set directly in each drawing cell; no tight bounding-box export changes the canvas scale.

Single plots (`S2.3`, `S2.8`, `S2.12`, `S2.15`) use 6-by-3.6 in canvases. The dense phonon/band comparisons (`fig2.5`, `S2.13`, `S2.14`) use 7-by-3.6 in to allow a narrow external legend without crowding the data. The original dual-horizontal-axis layout of `S2.2` uses 6-by-4.5 in; the FM/AFM DOS pair `S2.16` is stacked on a 6-by-6 in canvas. Band/DOS and phonon/band pairs use 8 in widths. The three-data-panel figures (`fig2.2`, `fig2.6`, `S2.1`, `S2.18`) use compact 8 in wide 2-by-2 layouts, with panels (a), (b), (c) in reading order and the shared legend in the lower-right quadrant. Six-panel optical figures use 10-by-5 in, except `fig2.11` at 10-by-5.3 in for its top legend; the gap distributions and AIMD composite remain full width with reduced heights.

Legends occupy an internal upper-right space only where it is amply clear. Dense phonon and band plots use a narrow right legend area. The six-panel absorption/loss figure uses a compact shared top row because its curves approach the available internal spaces. No legend is placed below the canvas. `S2.8` retains its legend inside a broad clear negative-frequency region on the right, leaving the actual left-hand negative branch unobscured. These exceptions avoid covering curves or crowding frames rather than adding empty plot ranges.

The five XCF-based structural figures require GIMP 3. The `fig2.1`, `fig2.3`, `fig2.4` and `fig2.7` XCF files remain in the sibling `PhD_thesis_20251216/figures_proj2` directory; `S2.17` uses this repository's `figures_collection/S2.17/S2.17_template.xcf`. Their existing paths work directly without moving or copying the artwork. Their original atom, unit-cell and isosurface artwork is retained; the helper temporarily hides text layers and replaces only the text in Matplotlib. These images are artwork, not a substitute for calculation data. Their widths remain 8 in for `fig2.1`, `fig2.3` and `fig2.4` (included at 0.8 thesis text width), and 7.5 in for `fig2.7` and `S2.17` (included at 0.75 text width). Thus their 11 pt labels and tabs print at the same scale as the numerical figures. `fig2.7` has 5.5% extra white space below the original image for its lower spin labels, without reducing or covering the physical artwork.

## Scientific identity and corrections

Optical curves come directly from the density-density diagonal tensors in `5.1_dielectric_function/*/vaspout.h5`. The retained effective thicknesses are 5.807056948202938, 9.41157 and 11.208620 Angstrom for monolayer, bilayer and H-bilayer. These are the adopted original effective-thickness convention, not a unique intrinsic out-of-plane dielectric definition. Absorption remains corrected to `alpha = 2 E k/(hbar c)` in Angstrom^-1; the old frequency/unit error is not reintroduced. HDF5 retains more digits than the previous XML extraction; the maximum difference after normalization is about 0.000207. The optical reference remains NM-PBE, with no added Drude model. The original dielectric plotting window capped at ±60 is retained where applicable.

HSE06 bands use `monolayer_FM_HSE06`, `monolayer_AFM_HSE06`, and `bilayer_HSE`, including `KPOINTS_OPT` and its Fermi reference. Every band/DOS run keeps its own original Fermi level. The `monolayer_FM_ollie` and `monolayer_AFM_ollie` DOSCAR/XML files are incomplete: their plotted DOS is reconstructed from OUTCAR eigenvalues and k weights. The Gaussian kernel retains the original helper's standard deviation of 0.05 eV. Both spin channels are now evaluated on one shared 4000-point absolute energy grid before their total is formed. The original helper independently generated the two spin grids, so plotting both against the spin-up grid displaced the spin-down spectrum. This is corrected without interpolating or changing eigenvalues. Each spin integrates to 32 states; an independent SciPy Gaussian mixture agrees to numerical precision (`spin_dos_verification.json`). This is not claimed to be a newly recovered complete DOS output.

The superconducting figure retains all 60 bulk and 52 H-bilayer files (5982 and 5092 saved histogram bins). The two-column EPW format encodes `T + rho/max(rho)` in column 1 and the gap in meV in column 2. Temperature is recovered from each filename; subtracting it from column 1 recovers the normalized density. This format is documented in the [official QE 6.8 EPW source](https://github.com/QEF/q-e/blob/qe-6.8/EPW/src/io_eliashberg.f90#L2034-L2082) and the [current legacy-format branch](https://github.com/QEF/q-e/blob/develop/EPW/src/io/io_supercond.f90#L3270-L3274); the exact locally used EPW version is not established. The previous plot and power-law fit incorrectly treated the density offset as temperature. The corrected figure puts every saved bin at the filename temperature, uses colour for the normalized density, and joins its weighted mean as a guide. The mean is `sum(rho * gap)/sum(rho)` on the original uniform 0.03 meV bins, with no interpolation or gap shift. Omitted bins do not justify trapezoidal integration across the remaining nonadjacent bins. No power-law fit, extrapolation or fitted critical-temperature line remains.

The bulk archive ends at 30 K with saved gap support 0.93–2.28 meV and mean 1.66799 meV. The H-bilayer archive ends at 26 K with a single saved bin at 0.03 meV, at the histogram resolution. Neither endpoint independently establishes a precise gap-closing temperature or a rigorous critical-temperature bracket: the archive contains no normal-state convergence output. The published 31.3 K and 24.3 K estimates are not newly validated by this figure. All 112 per-temperature bin counts, supports and density-weighted means agree with an independent parser to numerical precision.

The AIMD curves retain all 5500 original ionic steps from `6.0_AIMD/bilayer_H/OSZICAR`, with time step 1 fs and the electronic free-energy column `F`.

The common-grid spin-DOS repair and EPW histogram decoding are numerical corrections. The following label corrections are separate from the layout changes:

1. `S2.1(c)`: all 37 rows in `1.0_energy/o-B14/energy_lattice/energy_parameters.dat` specify the 10×14×9 grid and 600 eV cutoff. The previous caption's 34×48×31 did not describe this scan. Its lowest sampled lattice constant remains 5.70099 Angstrom. The original EOS plotting procedure and sampled energies are retained. The k-grid convergence panels use the first grid integer `n_x` (4 through 24) for readable ticks; each full mesh is preserved in the source table.
2. `S2.3`: the plotted `a3` column in `2.1_geometry_optimization/monolayer/energy_vacuum/energy_parameters.dat` is the total supercell height, not the vacuum-region thickness alone.
3. `S2.5`: `o-B14_n{32,64,128,256}_k10/OUTCAR` reports actual NBANDS of 48, 72, 144 and 264. These actual total band counts are the legend values; the directory/INCAR requests are 32, 64, 128 and 256.
4. `fig2.2`: original `thesis_demo.ipynb` uses the display names Group 1/Group 2/Group 3 for the blue `B2_index`, orange `B3_index` and purple `B7_index` selections. Its orange `s` curve is incorrectly labelled Group 3, while its orange `p` curve is labelled Group 2. The revised figure consistently uses G2/G3/G7 for those three selections, matching the B2/B3/B7 variable names and thesis structure labels. One-based atom selections remain G2 = 5–8; G3 = 1–4, 13, 14; G7 = all 14 atoms. These selections overlap and must not be described as three disjoint partitions. The original projected BZ segments are retained, with native 11 pt labels.

`fixed_spin_data.csv` is the small pre-existing thesis tabulation, copied here as the source of `S2.15.pdf`; no missing original DFT records have been invented. Original negative phonon frequencies remain visible: both `S2.9` panels extend to -3.3 THz, including the H-monolayer minimum of -2.871048372 THz. Both `S2.16` panels extend to 15 states/eV so that the AFM total-DOS maximum of 14.2567916276 is not clipped. These are display-range repairs, with no change to the underlying values. Nothing in the figure restyling resolves the previously documented H2 energy-accounting, QE/EPW-input or phonon-convergence gaps.
